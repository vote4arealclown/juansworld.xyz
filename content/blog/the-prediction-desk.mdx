A gambler wants to know who wins. I want to know what the number was — so I write it down before the game starts, and after the game there is a record instead of a story.

That single habit is the entire business model of saskpoly.xyz, the prediction desk I run. Everything else — the articles, the books, the machine that drafts for me — is downstream of it.

## Write it down before kickoff

Every pick the desk makes is published before the game, with the number it was made at. Every pick is graded after, against that published number: wins, losses, and pushes logged separately, never retroactively claimed, never quietly forgotten. When the desk loses — and the desk loses, the market sees to that — the loss goes in the first paragraph, with the math, on the same terms the pick was made.

This is rarer than it sounds. The internet is full of records that start the day the record-keeper got hot. A public ledger with your bad weeks still in it is a different kind of asset. It's the only kind I have.

## The voice is the product

The picks are free. They always will be. What's for sale is the process, and the process has a voice, and the voice has rules, and the rules are written down like code:

Numbers lead, narrative follows. One decimal place, stated plainly. Short declarative sentences. Then a fragment for rhythm.

No emojis. No exclamation marks. No "SNEAKY," no "you won't believe." Anyone selling you a bigger adjustment is selling a story.

Coin a term, define it, use it like it's vocabulary: fade budget, soft chalk, the team of death. Honesty as a flex — disclose the flaw before the reader finds it.

It sounds precious until you watch it work. A reader who trusts the numbers stops arguing with them. A reader who has watched you grade your own losses stops asking whether you're due.

## The machine behind the desk

I don't write the first draft of most desk pieces anymore. The loop — the clock-driven prediction machine behind the desk, which I wrote up separately — drafts them from verified facts and leaves them in a review folder. I edit every line before it publishes. The machine keeps the cadence; I keep the voice.

The best thing the machine ever did for the writing was give it something to be honest about. A desk that runs on a six-hour clock generates numbers faster than any pundit can hand-wave. The drafts are the commodity. The judgment is the job.

## The books

The process also fits in your pocket. Volume one of the Smart Bet Field Guide — NFL Survivor Pools — is on Amazon, and it's the whole method: how survivor pools actually work, where the equity leaks are, why fading the crowd is arithmetic and not attitude. Volume two is coming.

Buying the book is optional. Reading the desk is free. But if you want the system without the seasonal serialization, the book is the system.

Twenty legs, no rake, one Thanksgiving. Bring a pencil — and write the number down before kickoff, because after the game there is no story, only a record.
